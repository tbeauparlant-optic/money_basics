Financial Terms Glossary

Key financial concepts referenced in the Money Basics Student Guide.

Annual Percentage Rate (APR)

The yearly interest rate charged for borrowing or earned through an investment, expressed as a percentage of the amount borrowed or invested.

Assets

Anything of value owned by an individual or organization that can be converted into cash, such as property, savings, or investments.

ATM

An Automated Teller Machine that allows bank customers to withdraw cash, make deposits, or check account balances electronically.

Bank

A company chartered by state or federal government to offer financial services such as checking and savings accounts, loans, and safe-deposit boxes. The FDIC insures accounts in federally chartered banks and most state-chartered banks.

Bond

A certificate of indebtedness issued by a government or company, promising to repay borrowed funds to the lender with interest at predetermined intervals.

Borrower

An individual, business, or government that receives and uses something belonging to someone else with the intention of returning or repaying it, usually with interest.

Budget

A plan to manage income, spending, and saving over a specific period of time.

Capital Gains

A profit realized from the sale of property, stocks, or other investments.

Cash

Money in the form of paper currency or coins.

Certificate of Deposit (CD)

A certificate issued by a bank to a person depositing money for a specified period of time. Early withdrawals often incur a penalty.

Check

A written order to a financial institution directing it to pay a stated amount from the customer’s account.

Checking Account

A financial account into which people deposit money and from which they withdraw funds by writing checks or using debit or ATM cards.

Compound Interest

Interest calculated on both the initial principal and the accumulated interest from previous periods.

Credit

Borrowing money or obtaining goods or services with the promise to pay later, usually with interest.

Credit Card

A plastic card issued by a financial institution that allows the holder to borrow funds to pay for goods and services, with repayment required later.

Credit Report

A record collected by a credit agency that tracks a borrower’s credit history, including on-time and missed payments.

Credit Union

A not-for-profit financial cooperative owned by its members, offering many of the same services as banks, often with lower fees.

Debit Card

A plastic card linked to a checking account that allows the holder to pay for purchases or withdraw cash directly from their funds.

Dividend

A portion of a company’s profits paid to its shareholders, usually quarterly.

Expenses

Payments made for goods and services; money spent in the course of living or doing business.

Finance Charges

The cost of borrowing money, including interest and fees, on unpaid balances.

Financial Institutions

Organizations such as banks and credit unions that help channel funds from savers to borrowers.

Financial Literacy

Understanding financial systems, products, and behaviors — including budgeting, credit, saving, and investing — and applying that knowledge to make sound financial decisions.

Fixed Expenses

Costs that remain constant from month to month, such as rent or car payments.

Grace Period

The amount of time a borrower has to make a payment after its due date before penalties or interest are charged.

Human Capital

The knowledge, skills, and experience possessed by individuals that contribute to their economic value and earning potential.

Income

Money received through work, investments, or other sources.

Interest

The cost of borrowing money or the return on saving money, typically expressed as a percentage.

Lender

An individual or institution that loans money to a borrower with the expectation of repayment plus interest.

Liabilities

Financial obligations or debts owed to others, such as loans or credit balances.

Minimum Payment

The smallest amount a person must pay on a credit account in a given billing cycle to remain in good standing.

Money Market Account

A type of savings account that typically offers higher interest rates and limited check-writing privileges.

Mutual Fund

An investment vehicle that pools money from many investors to buy a diversified portfolio of stocks, bonds, or other assets.

Net Worth

The value of a person’s assets minus their liabilities; an overall measure of financial health.

Rate of Return

The gain or loss on an investment over a specified period, expressed as a percentage of the investment’s cost.

Return

The profit or income generated by an investment, usually stated as a percentage.

Risk

The chance of losing money or experiencing a financial loss on an investment or decision.

Rule of 72

A formula used to estimate the number of years required for an investment to double in value, found by dividing 72 by the annual interest rate.

Saving

Income not spent on current consumption or taxes; money set aside for future use.

Savings Account

An interest-bearing bank account used for storing money not intended for daily expenses.

Spending

Using income to purchase goods or services for current needs or wants.

Stocks

Shares of ownership in a corporation that represent a claim on part of its assets and earnings.

Taxes

Mandatory payments made to government to fund public programs and services.

Variable Expenses

Expenses that can change in amount or frequency, such as food, utilities, or entertainment.

Wealth

The accumulation of valuable resources and assets, such as savings, investments, and property.
