What Is a PDP?

A PDP is a tool used to assist people in making life changes. It focuses on an individual's strengths and guides them to identify their long-term goals and the short-term steps they can take to get there.

Person-directed planning is directed by the participant. The goal is to keep lived experiences, strengths, skills, goals, and dreams at the center of planning for the future.

Why Is It Useful?

Improving one's financial well-being is a personalized process. PDP helps participants identify tools, supports, and resources that work best for them, creating a sense of agency and empowerment.

Learning Goals

  • Create their own Person-Directed Plan
  • Create an Action Plan for achieving their financial goals

Instructions

In this module, participants complete a shortened version of the PDP worksheet. Encourage them to follow along with the Student and Facilitator Guides and to type answers directly into the worksheet fields.

Step 1: Life Now

Time: 3 minutes

Describe how your life is now. Pay special attention to your current financial picture and relationship with money.

  • What is your current source of income?
  • Who manages your finances?
  • What type of housing do you have?
  • Do you work? If so, where and how much do you make?
  • What debts are you aware of?

Step 2: What Works & What Doesn't

Time: 5 minutes

What works well for you? What makes things run smoothly? What causes problems currently?

Think about these questions specifically in regards to money and finances.

Step 3: Strengths, Gifts, Capacities

Time: 5 minutes

Identify your strengths, gifts, and capacities.

Strengths are valuable abilities or qualities (ex: good at math, writing, or public speaking).

Gifts are special talents or natural abilities (ex: compassionate, empathetic, good listener).

Capacities are areas of potential growth or accomplishment (ex: being a quick learner or strong communicator).

Some common transferable skills include communication, dependability, teamwork, critical thinking, organization, leadership, adaptability, and empathy.

Step 4: Financial Goals & Dreams

Time: 5 minutes

Describe your dreams and big goals for the next 5 to 10 years. Imagine your ideal future without barriers.

Step 5: Life One Year from Now

Time: 3 minutes

Imagine your life one year from now, knowing you are one year closer to your financial dreams.

Step 6: Action Plan

Time: 5 minutes

Create an Action Plan for achieving short-term and long-term financial goals. Focus on small steps that build toward a larger goal.
