Applying for Social Security Benefits

A guide for individuals experiencing behavioral health challenges, severe mental illness, or substance recovery.

Some individuals experiencing mental health and/or addiction challenges find that there are times when working becomes too difficult. Social Security Disability Insurance Benefits (SSDI) and Supplemental Security Income (SSI) are programs by the Social Security Administration (SSA) that provide monthly income and health insurance for people with qualifying conditions, which can include severe depression, bipolar disorder, PTSD, anxiety disorders, or substance use disorder (SUD).

Important note regarding medical evidence: The SSA has strict guidelines for qualifying that can be difficult to navigate. Generally, an individual needs to have detailed medical documentation that shows how their condition prevents them from working on a regular basis, and that they are actively undergoing treatment.
Eligibility Criteria (SSDI vs. SSI)

Social Security Disability Insurance (SSDI)

  • Work Requirement: Must have worked and paid into Social Security (payroll taxes/FICA) for at least 5 of the last 10 years.
  • Duration: Impairment must prevent working for at least 12 months.
  • Family Coverage: Spouse and children can receive your SSDI benefits.
  • Medicare: Eligible for Medicare health coverage after 24 months.

Supplemental Security Income (SSI)

  • Needs-Based: Must have very low income, resources, and assets.
  • Asset Limits: Cannot exceed $2,000 in assets for individuals ($3,000 for couples), excluding primary home and one vehicle.
  • State Supplements: May receive additional monthly payments from the state.
  • Medicaid: Eligible for immediate Medicaid health coverage.
Recommendations for Applying

It can be helpful to consult with your doctor, therapist, case manager, mentor, or trusted friend to help you navigate these criteria and gather all of the required information to apply for benefits. This will include:

  • All of your medical records (hospitalizations, therapy sessions, clinical assessments).
  • A written doctor or psychologist recommendation.
  • A list of all medications you are currently taking and any side effects.
  • Work history details and financial statements.

You can apply for SSDI or SSI online at www.ssa.gov ↗, over the phone, or in person at your local Social Security office. If you are approved, your case will be reviewed periodically.

Returning to Work (Ticket to Work)

If you decide to return to work, the SSA has a program called “Ticket to Work” that provides resources, training, and assistance. A Benefits Counselor can help you identify employment opportunities, explain how returning to work affects your SSI/SSDI benefits, and connect you to resources outside of the SSA. For more information, visit www.ssa.gov/work ↗.
